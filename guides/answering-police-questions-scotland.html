Do I have to answer police questions in Scotland?

The answer depends on why the police are speaking to you and whether you are in custody. This page focuses on the official Scottish custody guidance.

Core custody ruleScottish Government custody guidance says you have the right to remain silent. In police custody, however, you must provide your name, address, date of birth, place of birth and nationality when required.

Can I stay silent?

In police custody, official Scottish guidance says you do not have to answer questions about the suspected offence. Anything you choose to say may be recorded and could be used as evidence if the case goes to court.

What details do I have to give?

The Scottish Government Letter of Rights says you must give your name, where you live, date of birth, place of birth and nationality. If you do not know one of those details, tell the police.

Should I answer questions about the allegation?

That is a decision to make with legal advice. If you are a suspect or in custody, ask to speak privately with a solicitor before deciding what to say.

What if I am only being spoken to in the street?

Different legal powers can apply outside custody, including when police believe you are a suspect or witness. This page does not try to cover every street encounter. Ask the officer why they are speaking to you and get legal advice if you are unsure.
