Arrested: your rights in England & Wales

This starter guide summarises the official GOV.UK position on what normally happens after arrest.

Core pointIf you are arrested, you will usually be taken to a police station, held in custody and questioned. You may later be released or charged.

What usually happens after arrest?

GOV.UK says an arrested person will usually be taken to a police station, held in a cell and then questioned. After that, they may be released or charged with a crime.

What if I am under 18 or a vulnerable adult?

The police must arrange an appropriate adult to help protect your interests, rights and welfare. An appropriate adult can help you communicate and understand your rights, but they do not replace a solicitor and cannot give legal advice.

Can I speak privately to an appropriate adult?

GOV.UK says you can speak privately to your appropriate adult at any time. You can also decide whether they are present when you speak to a legal adviser.

Does this guide apply everywhere in the UK?

No. Scotland and Northern Ireland have separate rules and procedures.
