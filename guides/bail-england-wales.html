Bail after charge in England & Wales

Bail means being released from custody while waiting for a hearing or trial, sometimes subject to conditions.

Core pointYou can be given bail by police after charge or by a court. Conditions can be imposed to manage risks such as failing to attend court, committing offences or interfering with witnesses.

What conditions might be imposed?

Examples on GOV.UK include living at a particular address, not contacting certain people, surrendering a passport and reporting to a police station.

Can bail be refused?

Yes. A court can refuse bail in some circumstances, including where it believes you may not attend, may offend while on bail, or where other statutory grounds apply.

What if I break bail conditions?

You can be arrested again. The court may change the conditions or keep you in custody until the next stage of the case.
