What happens if police charge me in Scotland?

Being charged does not itself mean you are guilty. Police send reports to the procurator fiscal, who decides whether prosecution should proceed and what action is appropriate.

What can happen after charge?

Police may release you, release you on an undertaking with conditions, release you while the procurator fiscal decides what to do, or keep you in custody for the next court hearing. In some cases a warning or fixed penalty may be used instead.

Who decides if the case goes to court?

The procurator fiscal considers whether there is enough evidence and whether prosecution is in the public interest. The fiscal also decides which court and procedure should be used if the case proceeds.

Can I speak to a solicitor before court?

Yes. Official guidance says you are entitled to speak to a solicitor before appearing in court.
