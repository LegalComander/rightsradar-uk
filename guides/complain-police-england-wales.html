How to complain about police in England & Wales

This starter guide explains the basic police complaints route and the role of the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC).

Core pointThe IOPC oversees the police complaints system in England and Wales, but most complaints are handled by the police force or policing organisation concerned.

Where do I make the complaint?

You can complain directly to the relevant police force or use the complaint form on the IOPC website. If you submit through the IOPC website, the IOPC says the complaint is sent to the police force or organisation concerned for initial consideration.

Does the IOPC investigate every complaint?

No. The IOPC says it independently investigates only the most serious and sensitive matters. Most complaints are handled by the relevant force, often through its professional standards department.

What if I am unhappy with the outcome?

The IOPC explains that in some cases you can apply for a review or appeal if you are unhappy with the outcome or how the complaint was handled. The correct review body depends on the complaint.

What should I keep?

Keep dates, officer names or numbers if known, reference numbers, video or photographs, witness details and copies of correspondence. Clear records can help the complaint handler understand what happened.
