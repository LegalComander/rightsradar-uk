What happens in a criminal court case in Scotland?

Scottish criminal cases can be heard in different courts and under different procedures. This starter page explains the basic court structure, not the strategy for any individual case.

Court type mattersmygov.scot explains that the High Court of Justiciary hears the most serious criminal cases, while sheriff courts hear many other criminal cases under summary or solemn procedure.

Who decides whether a case goes to court?

mygov.scot says the Procurator Fiscal, the lawyer working for the prosecution, decides whether a case should go to court.

What is the High Court of Justiciary?

The High Court hears the most serious cases, including all rape and murder cases. Trials are heard by a judge and jury.

What happens in sheriff court?

Sheriff courts deal with many criminal cases. A solemn case is heard by a sheriff and jury. A summary case is heard by a sheriff without a jury.

What if I have been charged?

Do not use a general website to decide how to plead or conduct your defence. Speak to a criminal defence solicitor, especially if you have a court date or bail conditions.
