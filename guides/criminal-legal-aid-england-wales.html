Criminal legal aid in England & Wales

Legal aid can help with criminal legal advice and representation, but the rules differ between police-station advice and court proceedings.

Core pointLegal advice at the police station is free. If you are charged or have to go to court, a solicitor checks whether you qualify for criminal legal aid.

How is court legal aid decided?

The Legal Aid Agency uses rules that include the interests-of-justice test and, in many cases, financial eligibility. GOV.UK says some people qualify automatically for representation, including certain children, young people in full-time education and people receiving specified benefits.

Do I apply myself?

GOV.UK says a legal adviser applies for legal aid for you. You can speak to the same organisation that helped at the police station, ask about the court duty solicitor, or find a criminal legal aid solicitor.

Can contributions be required?

In some Crown Court cases, people who qualify can still be required to contribute depending on their finances. Your solicitor can explain how the means rules apply.
