Crown Court in England & Wales

The Crown Court deals with serious criminal cases, some cases sent up from a magistrates’ court, and certain appeals.

Core pointIn a Crown Court trial, the jury normally decides whether the defendant is guilty and the judge manages the trial and decides the sentence if there is a conviction.

What cases go there?

GOV.UK lists serious offences such as murder, rape and robbery, as well as cases sent from magistrates’ courts for trial or sentencing.

Does every Crown Court case have a jury?

Trials normally do. Other hearings, including some sentencing and appeal hearings, may be dealt with by a judge without a jury deciding guilt.

Can Crown Court decisions be appealed?

There may be appeal routes for convictions or sentences, but strict rules and time limits can apply. Get legal advice quickly if you are considering an appeal.
