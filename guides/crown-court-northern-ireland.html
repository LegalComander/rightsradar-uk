Crown Court criminal cases in Northern Ireland

The Crown Court tries offences charged on indictment — generally the more serious criminal offences.

Core pointThe Crown Court has jurisdiction over indictable trials. Some offences must be tried in the Crown Court, while some hybrid offences may be dealt with either summarily or on indictment.

Is there a jury?

Crown Court trials ordinarily involve a judge and jury, subject to any lawful exception applying to the particular case.

How does a case get there?

Serious cases can move from the Magistrates' Court through the applicable committal or transfer process before Crown Court proceedings.

What about appeals?

The Court of Appeal hears criminal appeals from the Crown Court. Appeal rights and deadlines are technical, so get legal advice quickly.

Official source used

Department of Justice NI — The Crown Court and Court of Appeal

Legal information, not legal adviceCrown Court cases can involve serious consequences. Use a qualified criminal solicitor and, where appropriate, counsel.
