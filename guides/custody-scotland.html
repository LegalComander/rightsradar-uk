Arrested or held in police custody in Scotland

Police custody is a high-stakes situation. This page highlights one of the most important safeguards and points you to the official information.

You can ask for a solicitorScottish Government and mygov.scot guidance says a person in police custody has the right to speak to a solicitor, including privately before questioning, and that advice/attendance while in police custody is free.

Before a police interview

mygov.scot says you can speak to a solicitor in private before questioning and have a solicitor in the room during questioning. It also explains that, once legal advice is requested, questioning normally waits until you have received it, subject to exceptional circumstances.

What if I already said no to a solicitor?

Official Scottish guidance says you can change your mind and ask for a solicitor later.

Does asking for a solicitor make me look guilty?

No. mygov.scot explicitly explains that speaking to a solicitor does not make it look like you have done something wrong.

What should I do if this is happening now?

Tell the police that you want legal advice and ask them to arrange a solicitor. For personalised advice about what you should or should not say, rely on the solicitor rather than a website.
