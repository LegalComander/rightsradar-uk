How long can police hold you in Scotland?

This page summarises the standard custody time limits described in the Scottish Government Letter of Rights. Special legal regimes can have different rules.

Standard custody limitThe Scottish Government says police can normally keep a person for up to 12 hours without charging them. This can be extended up to 24 hours if the required senior officer authorises it.

Can the police automatically keep me for 24 hours?

No. The Scottish Government guidance says the normal period is up to 12 hours. An extension up to 24 hours requires authorisation by a Police Inspector, or a Chief Inspector if the person is under 18.

Do I get a say about an extension?

The Letter of Rights says you have the right to have your say about the decision to extend custody, or you can choose to have your lawyer speak to the police for you.

Does this apply to every kind of detention?

No. Different statutory regimes can have different detention rules. For example, terrorism powers are governed separately. If you are being held now, ask what power is being used and speak to a solicitor.

What should I do if I am in custody now?

Ask for a solicitor. The official Scottish guidance says you can speak to a solicitor privately, and legal advice in police custody is free.
