Fingerprints, photographs & DNA in England & Wales

This starter guide explains the main GOV.UK rules on fingerprints, photographs and samples after arrest.

Core pointGOV.UK says police can take your photograph, fingerprints, a DNA sample such as a mouth swab or head-hair root, and swab the skin of your hands and arms without your permission.

Which samples need extra permission?

For samples such as blood or urine, or dental impressions, GOV.UK says police normally need both your permission and the authority of a senior police officer. There are separate rules for blood or urine taken in connection with drink or drug driving.

What if I am under 18 or vulnerable?

GOV.UK says an appropriate adult must be with you when fingerprints, photographs or samples are taken if you are under 18 or a vulnerable adult.

Where is the information stored?

Fingerprint and sample information can be stored on a police database. GOV.UK explains that you can ask your local police force about records it holds and, in limited circumstances, ask for personal information to be removed.

Can I always get it deleted?

No. GOV.UK describes only limited situations in which personal information may be removed, including where an offence no longer exists or something in the police process was unlawful. The exact retention rules can depend on the case.
