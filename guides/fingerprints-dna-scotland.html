Can police take my fingerprints or DNA in Scotland?

If you are arrested in Scotland, police have powers to take certain identification and forensic material. The rules are not identical for every kind of sample.

Fingerprints, photographs and mouth swabs

mygov.scot says police can take photographs, fingerprints and certain DNA samples after arrest without your permission, and may use reasonable force if you refuse.

Blood, urine and intimate procedures

Official guidance says a warrant is generally needed for blood or urine samples, with exceptions such as drink or drug driving. Warrants or additional authorisation can also be required for intimate or strip-search procedures.

How long can data be kept?

Retention depends on what happens in the case and the type of offence. The official page explains situations where material is destroyed, kept for a fixed period or retained permanently following conviction.
