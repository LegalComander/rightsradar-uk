Free legal advice at the police station

This starter guide explains the right to free legal advice when you are questioned at a police station in England and Wales.

Core pointIf you are questioned at a police station, you have a right to free legal advice. GOV.UK says you must be told about this right after arrest and before questioning.

How can I get free legal advice?

You can ask for the police station duty solicitor, ask the police to arrange legal advice through the Defence Solicitor Call Centre, or ask the police to contact a solicitor you choose.

Is the duty solicitor independent?

Yes. GOV.UK says the duty solicitor is available 24 hours a day and is independent of the police.

What if I first say no?

You can change your mind later. The official guidance says you still have the right to free legal advice if you initially turn it down.

Can police question me after I ask for legal advice?

Usually they must wait until you have received legal advice, although GOV.UK identifies exceptions. In serious cases a senior officer can authorise a delay.

What if I am under 18 or a vulnerable adult?

An appropriate adult can arrange legal advice for you even if you do not want it.
