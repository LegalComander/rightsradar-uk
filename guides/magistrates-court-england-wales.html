Magistrates’ Court in England & Wales

All criminal cases start in a magistrates’ court. Some stay there; more serious cases can move to the Crown Court.

Core pointCases are heard by magistrates or a District Judge. There is no jury in a magistrates’ court.

What cases does it deal with?

Magistrates’ courts normally deal with summary offences and can also deal with some either-way offences. The most serious indictable offences are sent to the Crown Court.

Can the court decide bail?

Yes. The magistrates’ court may decide whether you are released on bail or kept in custody until another hearing.

What happens if my case is too serious?

The case can be sent to the Crown Court for trial or sentencing, depending on the offence and what has happened in the case.
