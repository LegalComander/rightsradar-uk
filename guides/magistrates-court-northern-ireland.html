Criminal cases in the Magistrates' Court in Northern Ireland

The Magistrates' Court deals with many adult criminal cases and plays a role in the early stages of more serious cases.

Core pointIn a summary prosecution, a District Judge decides guilt or innocence. If the defendant pleads guilty, sentencing may happen immediately or later; a not-guilty plea leads toward a contested hearing.

Who decides the case?

Adult summary criminal cases are heard by a District Judge without a jury.

What about serious cases?

More serious indictable cases can involve committal procedures in the Magistrates' Court before proceeding to the Crown Court, subject to current statutory arrangements.

Should I have a solicitor?

Legal representation can be especially important for plea, bail, evidence and sentencing decisions. Ask a solicitor about criminal legal aid.
