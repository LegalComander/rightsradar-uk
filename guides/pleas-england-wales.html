Guilty or not guilty pleas in England & Wales

When a criminal case reaches court, the defendant may be asked to enter a plea to the charge.

Core pointA guilty plea means accepting the offence and normally leads to conviction and sentencing. A not guilty plea means the case proceeds toward a trial where the prosecution must prove the case.

Should I get legal advice before pleading?

Yes. HM Courts & Tribunals Service guidance tells defendants to speak to a lawyer before the hearing. A plea can have major consequences, so do not guess about what you should say.

Where will the trial happen?

That depends on the offence. Summary cases are normally dealt with in the magistrates’ court; the most serious indictable cases go to the Crown Court; some either-way cases can be tried in either court.

What happens after a guilty plea?

The court moves to sentencing, either immediately or at a later hearing if more information is needed.
