Police questioning in England & Wales

This starter guide explains the basic rules in plain English. It links to the official GOV.UK guidance and PACE Code C for the full detail.

Core pointPolice may question you about the offence they suspect you of. The interview is normally recorded. You do not have to answer questions, but the police caution warns that there can be consequences if you later rely in court on something you did not mention when questioned.

What caution should I hear?

GOV.UK says police must explain your rights by reading the police caution before questioning. In plain terms, it says you do not have to speak, but staying silent about something you later rely on in court may harm your defence, and anything you do say may be used in evidence.

Can I get a solicitor first?

Yes. People in police custody have the right to free legal advice. Asking for legal advice does not make you look guilty. If you want advice before an interview, say so clearly.

Does this apply to voluntary interviews?

PACE Code C says people attending voluntarily to assist with an investigation should have no less consideration and have an absolute right to obtain legal advice. The detailed rules depend on the circumstances.

Should I answer questions without advice?

This site cannot tell you what to say in a particular case. Because silence can have legal consequences in some circumstances, get advice from a solicitor before deciding how to answer police questions.
