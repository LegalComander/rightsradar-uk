Masks, balaclavas and face coverings at protests

A face covering is not automatically unlawful at every protest. The legal position depends on why it is being used, where you are, and whether police have made a valid designation.

Core pointUnder the Crime and Policing Act 2026, it is an offence to wear or otherwise use an item that conceals identity in a public place within a designated protest locality. Police can only make that designation when the statutory protest-and-criminality conditions are met.

What counts as concealing identity?

The law is broad enough to cover something worn over the face, such as a mask or balaclava, and also another item used to hide identity, such as holding an object close to the face.

When can police designate an area?

A designation can be made where police reasonably believe a protest is or may be taking place, protest activity is likely to involve or has involved offences, and designation would help prevent or control offending. The designation is time-limited and the public must be notified.

Are there exceptions?

Yes. The 2026 offence contains defences for coverings used for health reasons, religious observance or work. The precise facts still matter.

What is the maximum penalty?

The statutory maximum is one month’s imprisonment, a level 3 fine (currently £1,000), or both.

What about the older section 60AA power?

Section 60AA of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 already allows a uniformed constable, where the relevant authorisation is in force, to require removal of an item reasonably believed to be worn wholly or mainly to conceal identity, and to seize items intended for that purpose. Failing to comply can itself be an offence.

Does this law apply across the whole UK?

No. The new Crime and Policing Act 2026 protest face-covering offence generally extends and applies to England and Wales, not Scotland or Northern Ireland. Use the jurisdiction-specific protest guides for those areas.
