Locking-on and disruptive protest tactics

The Public Order Act 2023 created specific offences aimed at highly disruptive protest tactics and expanded police stop-and-search powers.

Core pointLocking-on can be an offence where the statutory elements are met, including serious disruption. Separate offences cover going equipped to lock on, tunnelling, major transport works and key national infrastructure.

Locking-on

The offence can cover attaching yourself, another person or an object to a person, object or land in a place other than a dwelling where the conduct causes or is capable of causing serious disruption and the required mental element is present. A statutory reasonable-excuse defence exists.

Going equipped to lock on

It is also an offence to have an object with you in a place other than a dwelling with the required intention that it may be used in connection with locking-on.

Tunnelling

The Act created offences relating to causing serious disruption by tunnelling, being present in a tunnel and being equipped for tunnelling. The precise elements and defences differ between offences.

Transport and national infrastructure

There are separate offences for obstructing major transport works and interfering with key national infrastructure such as certain transport, energy and other nationally important systems.

Protest stop and search

Police have suspicion-led powers to search for items intended for protest-related offences. Section 11 of the 2023 Act also permits suspicionless searches in an authorised area where the statutory test is met. PACE Code A sets safeguards for these searches.

Road obstruction and public nuisance

Other offences may apply independently, including wilful obstruction of a highway and intentionally or recklessly causing public nuisance. Whether peaceful protest provides a defence or affects proportionality depends on the particular offence and facts.

Serious Disruption Prevention Orders

The Act also created Serious Disruption Prevention Orders, which can impose requirements or prohibitions on qualifying individuals under the statutory scheme.
