New protest laws introduced in 2026

The Crime and Policing Act 2026 made a substantial set of public-order changes in England and Wales. The relevant provisions came into force in June 2026.

Key changeThe Act added new protest-specific offences and strengthened police powers. These provisions generally apply to England and Wales rather than automatically across the whole UK.

Concealing identity

A new offence applies to wearing or otherwise using an item that conceals identity in a public place inside a police-designated protest locality. Health, religious and work-related defences are expressly provided.

Pyrotechnics

The Act created a protest-specific offence relating to possession of pyrotechnic articles, such as flares and fireworks, by people taking part in protests.

Specified memorials

The Act created an offence concerning climbing on specified war and other memorials of national significance. It does not turn climbing every statue or structure into this specific offence; the statutory list and circumstances matter.

Protests outside homes

New provisions address protests outside the homes of public-office holders and expand existing powers concerning harassment, alarm or distress near homes.

Places of worship

Police powers to impose protest conditions were strengthened where a protest near a place of worship may create an intimidating atmosphere that deters people from accessing the place for religious activity.

Cumulative disruption

Senior officers must take account of the combined effect of repeated or sustained protest activity when considering conditions under sections 12 and 14 of the Public Order Act 1986.

Who can impose conditions?

The Act allows appropriately authorised senior commanders, including commanders working away from the immediate protest scene, to impose conditions on live processions and assemblies in addition to the senior officer at the scene.

BTP and Ministry of Defence Police

The Act also expanded specified public-order powers available to the British Transport Police and Ministry of Defence Police within their jurisdictions.
