Protest rights and public processions in Northern Ireland

The legal framework in Northern Ireland is distinct. Public processions and protests related to parades are regulated through the Public Processions (Northern Ireland) Act 1998 and the Parades Commission, alongside general criminal law and PSNI powers.

Core pointA public procession normally must be notified at least 28 days in advance. A protest meeting related to a parade normally must be notified at least 14 days in advance.

Public processions

Organisers of public processions generally must notify the police using the prescribed process at least 28 days before the event, unless that is not reasonably practicable. The information is passed to the Parades Commission.

Parade-related protest meetings

A protest organised in opposition to or in relation to a notified parade is subject to a separate notification process. The Parades Commission states that the normal notice period is at least 14 days before the parade.

Can the Parades Commission impose conditions?

Yes. The Commission can issue legally binding determinations imposing conditions on public processions and related protest meetings. Organisers and participants should check the determination applying to their event.

What does PSNI say about lawful protest?

PSNI states that the right to participate in public processions and lawful protest must be balanced against public safety, the rights of others and the prevention of crime. Police may give lawful instructions, gather evidence, warn participants and arrest where an offence is suspected.

Roads, buildings and obstruction

PSNI guidance says protesters must not endanger people, block a roadway or footpath, enter buildings as trespassers, damage property, or intentionally interfere with, impede or obstruct lawful activity. The precise offence and available defence depend on the facts.

Masks and balaclavas

The specific Crime and Policing Act 2026 protest face-covering offence introduced in England and Wales does not generally extend to Northern Ireland. Other criminal laws, event conditions and police powers can still apply depending on conduct and circumstances.
