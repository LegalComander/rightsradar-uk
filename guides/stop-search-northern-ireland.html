Stopped and searched by police in Northern Ireland

PSNI can use statutory stop-and-search powers in specified circumstances. The power used determines whether reasonable suspicion is required.

Core pointPSNI says most searches require reasonable grounds, but some authorised powers can operate without reasonable suspicion. Being searched does not by itself mean you are under arrest.

What should happen during the search?

Officers must follow the applicable code of practice and use the power fairly, lawfully and proportionately. PSNI says the search should be kept as short as reasonably possible and usually take place near where you were stopped.

Do I have to stay?

If a lawful stop-and-search power is being exercised, you are detained for the duration of the search and must remain until the officer says the search is complete or you are free to leave.

Can I get a record?

Where a stop-and-search record is made, PSNI says the person searched can request a copy within 12 months.
