How do I get my stop and search record?

Police search records can matter if you later want to understand or challenge what happened.

Core pointPACE Code A says a person who was not given an immediate copy may request a copy of the stop-and-search record within three months of the search.

Will I always get paper at the scene?

If it is practicable to make the record on the spot and you request it, police may provide a copy or a receipt explaining how to obtain the full record or access an electronic version.

What if I was arrested after the search?

If you are arrested and taken to a police station, the search record may form part of your custody record. You can ask for a copy of the custody record in accordance with the rules.

How long do I have?

For ordinary PACE stop-and-search records, Code A says you may request a copy within three months from the date of the search. Some specialist terrorism-search provisions have different record entitlements.

Why keep the record?

It can help preserve details such as the legal power used, grounds, date, time and officer information if you later seek advice or make a complaint.
