Stopped and searched by police in Scotland

This starter guide explains the basic framework in plain English. It is deliberately short and links you to the official sources for the full rules.

Core pointPolice Scotland says an officer must have a specific reason and use a specific legal power to search you. Its public guidance also says officers should explain why you have been stopped and what they are looking for.

Does being stopped mean I am arrested?

No. Police Scotland's public guidance says being stopped and searched does not, by itself, mean that you are under arrest.

What is the legal framework?

The Scottish Government's Stop and Search Code of Practice sets the framework for searches of people who are not in police custody. The Code says stop-and-search activity must be lawful, necessary and proportionate.

What should the officer explain?

Police Scotland's guidance says officers should explain why they have stopped you and, before the search, what they are looking for.

What if I think the search was improper?

Do not rely on this page alone. Record what you can remember, keep any paperwork, and consider getting advice from a solicitor or an appropriate advice service. The exact remedy depends on the facts.
