Young & vulnerable people in police custody

This starter guide explains some of the extra safeguards in PACE Code C for children and vulnerable people.

Core pointPACE Code C requires an appropriate adult for juveniles and for people treated as vulnerable under the Code. Their role is to help safeguard the person's rights, welfare and understanding during police procedures.

Who can be an appropriate adult?

For a child, this can include a parent, guardian, a representative of the organisation responsible for the child, a social worker, or another suitable independent adult aged 18 or over. For a vulnerable adult, it may be a relative, guardian, carer, someone experienced in supporting vulnerable people, or another suitable independent adult.

What does the appropriate adult do?

PACE Code C says the appropriate adult supports, advises and assists the person, helps them understand their rights, helps communication with police and can raise concerns if they believe the police are not acting properly or fairly.

What extra protections apply to under-18s?

GOV.UK says an under-18 should not be put in a cell unless there is nowhere else safe and comfortable, and must never be placed in a cell with an adult. PACE Code C also contains detailed rules about care, detention and interviews.

What about legal advice?

An appropriate adult is not a replacement for a solicitor. PACE Code C makes clear that juveniles and vulnerable people should have the opportunity to consult privately with a solicitor if they want legal advice.

What if vulnerability is not obvious?

PACE Code C requires police to consider vulnerability case by case. A person can be vulnerable because of a mental health condition or other factors affecting their ability to understand, communicate or cope with police procedures.
