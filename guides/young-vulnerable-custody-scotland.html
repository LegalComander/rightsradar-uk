Extra safeguards for young or vulnerable people in Scottish police custody

Children, young people and vulnerable adults can have extra safeguards in police custody. The exact rule depends on age, supervision status and communication needs.

Under 16s and some 16–17 year olds

Scottish Government guidance says police must try to tell a parent or guardian when a person is under 16, or is 16 or 17 and subject to certain compulsory supervision arrangements. A lawyer generally must be present for interview unless exceptional circumstances apply.

Vulnerable adults

Police can arrange an Appropriate Adult where a person needs help to understand or communicate. The Appropriate Adult supports communication but does not provide legal advice.

Can an Appropriate Adult replace a solicitor?

No. An Appropriate Adult and a solicitor have different roles. In some cases a vulnerable person cannot consent to interview without a solicitor present.
