Protest rights across the UK

Peaceful protest is protected, but the rules are different in England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. Use the guide for the jurisdiction where the protest takes place.

ImportantDo not assume a protest rule from one part of the UK applies in another. England and Wales received major new public-order provisions in the Crime and Policing Act 2026 which generally do not extend to Scotland or Northern Ireland.

Face coverings and balaclavas

England and Wales now has a specific protest-related identity-concealment offence for designated areas. That provision does not generally apply in Scotland or Northern Ireland. Always check the jurisdiction-specific page.

Flags, signs, chants and proscribed groups

Terrorism law can apply to inviting support for a proscribed organisation or displaying articles in circumstances that create the statutory suspicion of membership or support. Read the flags, signs and chants guide →

Safe access zones around abortion services

All three UK legal systems restrict specified protest, influencing, obstruction or harassment near abortion services, but they use different legislation and zone sizes. Compare the safe access zone rules →

What this section covers

The guides cover the practical rules most likely to affect people attending or organising protests: advance notification, police conditions, road obstruction, serious disruption, locking-on, stop-and-search powers, face coverings, pyrotechnics, safe access zones, terrorism-support restrictions, public-order offences, arrests and complaints.

Legal information, not legal advicePublic-order law is fact-sensitive and police directions can change during a live event. If you are arrested, charged or given a court order, obtain advice from a qualified solicitor.
