Scotland: police and criminal court rights

This page expands RightsRadar UK’s Scotland library with practical explanations based on current Scottish Government, mygov.scot, Police Scotland and Scottish Courts material.

Use the official sourceEach section links to the public authority used to prepare it. For an active case, arrest, charge or court date, get advice from a qualified Scottish solicitor.

When can police arrest me in Scotland?

Police need a lawful basis to arrest. Official guidance says this can include reasonable grounds to suspect involvement in a crime, enough evidence to charge, or an arrest warrant.

  • Police should identify themselves, tell you that you are under arrest, explain the suspected crime and why arrest is necessary.
  • Police powers of arrest can be used on the street, at home or at work.

Official source: mygov.scot — Powers of arrest →

Can police take my fingerprints or DNA?

After arrest, Police Scotland can take certain identification and forensic material without your permission.

  • Official guidance covers photographs, fingerprints and some DNA samples such as mouth swabs.
  • Different rules apply to blood, urine, intimate searches and retention of biometric data.

Official source: mygov.scot — Fingerprints, photographs, samples and searches →

What happens if I am charged?

Being charged does not mean you are guilty. Police normally report the case to the procurator fiscal, who decides whether prosecution should proceed.

  • You may be released, released on an undertaking, or kept in custody for court depending on the circumstances.
  • You are entitled to speak to a solicitor before appearing in court.

Official source: mygov.scot — Being charged with a crime →

What is a police undertaking?

An undertaking can allow a charged person to leave police custody before the first court hearing, subject to conditions.

  • Conditions can include attending court, not committing offences and not interfering with witnesses or evidence.
  • Further conditions can be challenged; speak to a solicitor about an appeal or asking the procurator fiscal to relax them.

Official source: mygov.scot — Being charged with a crime →

What is investigative liberation?

Police can release a suspect while continuing an investigation and impose temporary conditions.

  • mygov.scot says these conditions cannot last longer than 28 days.
  • You can appeal conditions to a sheriff court. Breaching a condition is a criminal offence.

Official source: mygov.scot — How long you can be held in custody →

Can I get criminal legal aid?

Criminal legal aid can help with solicitor and court-representation costs if you meet the relevant tests.

  • Financial circumstances and the type or seriousness of case can affect eligibility.
  • A solicitor who carries out legal-aid work can advise you and submit the application.

Official source: mygov.scot — Criminal cases: legal aid →

How do I complain about Police Scotland?

You can complain if Police Scotland did not provide the service you expected or acted unfairly or incorrectly.

  • If you are unhappy with how the complaint was handled, you can ask the independent Police Investigations and Review Commissioner (PIRC) for a review.
  • mygov.scot, updated 17 June 2026, says a PIRC application should normally be sent within 3 months of Police Scotland sending its findings.

Official source: mygov.scot — Complain about the police →

What extra safeguards apply to young or vulnerable people?

Young people and vulnerable adults can have extra custody safeguards depending on age, supervision status and communication needs.

  • Police may need to notify a parent or guardian for younger people.
  • A vulnerable adult may receive Appropriate Adult communication support; that does not replace legal advice from a solicitor.

Official source: mygov.scot — Young people and vulnerable adults →

Medical help, interpreters and communication support

Official Scottish custody guidance includes rights to practical support while detained.

  • You can ask for medical help if you are unwell.
  • You can get an interpreter if you do not speak or understand English, and communication help may be available if you are deaf or struggle to understand what is happening.

Official source: mygov.scot — When you are arrested →

What does guilty or not guilty mean?

At the start of a Scottish criminal case the accused is asked to plead to the charge or charges.

  • After a not-guilty plea, a trial date is fixed so evidence can be heard.
  • Scottish Courts guidance says an accused person can plead guilty at any stage. Get advice before entering a plea.

Official source: Scottish Courts — Criminal court procedures →

Can I get a stop-and-search record?

Police Scotland says you may be entitled to a copy of your stop-and-search information.

  • The request should be made within six months of the search date.
  • There are exceptions, including certain searches after arrest and some searches where identifying details were not provided.

Official source: Police Scotland — Your Stop and Search Information →
